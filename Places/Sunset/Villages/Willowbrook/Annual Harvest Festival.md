# Overview
The Harvest Festival taking place in [[Willowbrook]] ever year, after the first harvest
# Activities
## Harvest King and Queen Contest
Participants dress up in their finest harvest-themed attire and parade around the village square.

**Judging Criteria:**
- Creativity
- Craftsmanship
- Overall presentation

**Prize:**
- The winners are crowned Harvest King and Queen and receive a special prize (a handcrafted crown, a basket of local delicacies, or a small amount of gold).
- **Participation:** 10 XP per player for participating in the contest.
- **Winning the Title:** 50 XP for the player who wins either Harvest King or Queen.
## Festival Performances
Local bards, musicians, and actors perform throughout the day, providing entertainment for all attendees.

**Performances:**
- Folk music and dances
- Short plays and skits
- Storytelling sessions featuring local legends and myths

**Interaction:**
- Players can join in the performances, earning small rewards and gaining favor with the villagers.

**Rewards:**
- **Joining a Performance:** 10 XP per player for actively participating in a performance (e.g., playing music, dancing, acting).
- **Outstanding Performance:** 25 XP for a performance that impresses the crowd (e.g., a successful high DC Performance check, or roleplaying creativity).
## Festival Market
Stalls are set up where villagers and visitors can buy and sell homemade goods, crafts, and produce.
**Rewards:**
- **Haggling or Bartering:** 5 XP per successful bartering interaction or haggling for a better price.
- **Uncovering a Secret:** 25 XP for discovering hidden clues or secrets while interacting with stall owners (e.g., following up on the faded painting clue)

## Pie eating contest

A classic competition where participants try to eat as many pies as possible within a set time.

**Rules:**
- Each round lasts 1 minute.
- Contestants must make a Constitution saving throw (DC 10) each round.

**Chance Table:**

| Dice Roll | Outcome                                                                                                          |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1-5       | The contestant gets a pie in the face, no pies eaten this round.                                                 |
| 6-10      | The contestant eats 1 pie successfully.                                                                          |
| 11-15     | The contestant eats 2 pies successfully.                                                                         |
| 16-19     | The contestant eats 3 pies successfully.                                                                         |
| 20        | The contestant eats 4 pies successfully and gains inspiration (advantage on one roll) for their impressive feat. |
**Rewards:**
- **Participation:** 10 XP for entering the contest.
- **Winning a Round:** 25 XP for winning a round of the pie-eating contest.
- **Winning the Entire Contest:** 50 XP for winning the overall contest.

## Meet the end's 

#### Objective

The goal is to roll dice and get as close to a target number (the "ends") as possible without exceeding it. The player who gets the closest to the ends without going over wins the pot of coins.

#### Setup
1. **Determine the Ends:**
   - Each round, one player rolls 2d12 and adds the scores together. This sum is the "ends" that all players need to try to meet.
   - Example: If the rolls are 7 and 5, the ends are 12.
2. **Create the Pot:**
   - Each player contributes a set number of coins to the pot. The number of coins is equal to the ends.
   - Example: If the ends are 12, each player throws 12 coins into the pot.

#### Playing the Game
1. **Rolling the Dice:**
   - Players take turns rolling a d6 up to six times, trying to get as close to the ends as possible without exceeding it.
  - Players can stop rolling at any time before their six rolls are up if they are satisfied with their score.
2. **Disqualification:**
   - If a player’s total score from the dice rolls exceeds the ends, they are disqualified for that round.
3. **Example Turn:**
  - The ends are 14.
  - Player 1 rolls a 6, another 6, and then a 5. Their total is 17, which is higher than 14, so they are disqualified.
   - Player 2 rolls a 4, a 3, a 2, and another 3, getting a total of 12. They decide to stop rolling since they are close to 14 but not over.
   - Player 3 rolls a 6, another 6, and a 2, hitting exactly 14, meeting the ends perfectly and winning the pot.

#### Winning
- The player who meets the ends exactly wins the pot.
- If more than one player meets the ends, those players roll against each other in a new round without adding more coins to the pot.
- If no player meets the ends, the player with the closest score under the ends wins the pot.
- If all players exceed the ends and disqualify themselves, everyone replays the round but must throw more coins into the pot.

**XP Rewards**
- **Participation:** 5 XP per round of play.
- **Winning a Round:** 20 XP for winning a round by meeting the ends.
- **Winning Multiple Rounds:** 50 XP for winning three or more rounds during the festival.
#### Summary

1. Roll 2d12 to determine the ends.
2. Each player contributes coins equal to the ends into the pot.
3. Players take turns rolling a d6 up to six times, aiming to get as close to the ends as possible without exceeding it.
4. The closest player to the ends without going over wins the pot.
5. If everyone exceeds the ends, replay the round with additional coins added to the pot.


## Borin's Gambit

Card game - 10g buy in - Roll 1d8 for each player, keeping the die h8dden.

Each player can raise the bet, call the bet (meet it) or fold. It continues when all bets are equal. Then each player rolls a 1d6, keeping it a secret as well. A final round to raise, call or fold. Each remaining player rolls 1d4. Reveal the 1d8, 1d6 and 1d4. Add them all up together. Winner takes 80% of the pot (20% to game host). Ties split the 80%. 
- Sleight of hand for reroll. Deception can force a fold


## Lizard Racing
10g buy in, can bet more

5 lizards are released in a small track. Roll 1d4 for each lizard three times, describing the stages of the race at each round of rolls. Lizard with highest rolls wins. Who bet on it doubles their bet as winnings. Second place bets gets half of their bet back.
